# Referee 2 — Contribution / Framing / External Validity (agent) — 2026-06-19

**Target journal (calibration):** Top general-interest (AER / JPE / QJE bar)
**Recommendation:** Major Revision
**Composite score:** 62 / 100
**Realistic fit:** *Journal of Public Economics* / *Journal of Urban Economics*; AEJ:Economic Policy (aspirational stretch); **not** AER/QJE/JPE in current form.

## Headline verdict

A genuinely novel and well-executed national measurement of the off-market intra-family
housing channel is stapled to a single-state, statistically fragile (p≈0.10), and
admittedly-unobserved-supply Prop 19 evaluation. As one paper aimed at a top-5, the causal
half drags down the descriptive half, and the welfare / "supply of American homes" framing
outruns what the deeds actually show. The measurement contribution is a strong field-journal
paper on its own; the Prop 19 piece is a separate field paper, not a top-5 causal anchor.
**The honest move is to decide which paper this is.**

## Strengths

1. **The descriptive contribution is real and, to my knowledge, genuinely first:** a
   national, deeds-microdata taxonomy of 144M events isolating ~20M inter-person family
   transfers, with a disciplined lower-bound (same-surname) measure and a clean
   falsification (same-person retitles tracking trust self-transfers almost exactly). This
   is more than the cited antecedents (Delventhal industry tabulation, heirs'-property
   subset, LAO CA-only counts) deliver.
2. **Table 1 taxonomy validation is persuasive on its own fingerprints** (90% zero-price,
   40% quitclaim, family-discount median prices, near-absent corporate buyers) — it directly
   answers the obvious "these are disguised sales" objection.
3. **The bunching evidence (Fig 4)** — Feb 2021, normally the quietest month, becomes the
   busiest in the series; +31% over a transparent counterfactual — is the cleanest single
   result and is hard to attribute to a slow-moving confound.
4. **Intellectual honesty has improved markedly since v1:** the paper now states its
   single-treated-cluster problem, reports permutation p-values, reframes magnitudes as
   bounds, and openly labels the supply effect "deferred" and the conditional margins
   "statistically unremarkable." Preserve this candor.
5. **The "where are the missing homes?" direct test** is a smart, falsifiable move that
   turns an apparent null into an interpretation (inter-vivos deferral) rather than hiding it.

## CRITICAL

- **The two-papers problem (central to a top-5 verdict).** The national-measurement half and
  the CA-causal half do not share one load-bearing thesis. Measurement punchline: "the
  family is a large, sticky, off-market allocation channel." Prop 19 punchline: "a CA tax
  change shifted recorded family-deed volume." The model (eq. 2) is the only connective
  tissue, and it is decoration rather than contribution — a standard tax-capitalization
  keep-or-sell wedge that is never estimated, calibrated, or used to recover a structural
  object. At the AER/QJE/JPE bar this reads as two field papers sharing a CoreLogic extract.
  **NEW vs v1** (prior reviews were methods/code-focused). *What would change my mind:*
  either (a) split into two papers, or (b) make the model do real work — calibrate the wedge
  t(p−A₀) to CA assessment-roll data and show the predicted volume response quantitatively
  matches the estimated one.
- **Overclaim in the title and welfare framing.** "The Supply of American Homes" is not
  supported. The paper measures recorded deeds and a volume decline; by its own direct test,
  the supply release is unobserved and "deferred," and the national reading explicitly says
  no national reform would move a quarter of the channel. "Misallocation à la
  Glaeser–Luttmer" is asserted, not measured — no surplus calculation, no estimate of u_h vs
  u_m dispersion, no deadweight-loss number. *What would change my mind:* retitle to claim
  what is shown (non-market transfers and the *circulation/turnover* of homes), and either
  produce a bounded welfare magnitude with stated assumptions, or demote the misallocation
  language to clearly-flagged conjecture.
- **Inferential ceiling on the causal claim.** Headline causal estimate rests on p≈0.10
  (gross) / 0.18 (net), single treated state, with the larger placebo declines in CA's
  boom-bust Western neighbors — precisely the states the design most fears. The DDD
  supply-release test is p=0.67; the direct estate-sale test p=0.71/0.63. A top-5 causal
  contribution cannot be anchored on an effect that ranks 5th of 51 whose
  mechanism-confirming tests are all insignificant. The *machinery* is now correct (v1 C1
  addressed); what is not resolved is that, done correctly, the inference is weak. *What
  would change my mind:* a within-CA design identified off treatment intensity (county
  pre-reform assessment-gap / long-held-parcel-share × reform date), or honest reframing of
  Prop 19 as suggestive corroboration rather than the causal centerpiece.

## MAJOR

- **External validity is structurally limited and half-admitted.** The causal wedge requires
  acquisition-value assessment with a large p−A₀ gap, which the paper itself calls "the
  extreme" and largely a CA phenomenon. The policy lesson generalizes to a handful of
  acquisition-value/assessment-cap states, not to "American homes." *Fix:* a scope sentence
  in abstract + intro delimiting the causal claim, plus a back-of-envelope of how many
  states/parcels are in that regime.
- **Novelty needs sharper differentiation from Delventhal/Cotality (2026)** and the
  heirs'-property papers. Delventhal already tabulates inheritance deeds nationally
  (~340k/yr). *Fix:* a direct reconciliation showing exactly what Delventhal counts vs what
  this paper counts (inter-vivos gifts they miss, retention tracking they cannot do).
- **The KM "family lock" fact (62% unsold at 10y) is pooled** across states/cohorts; the
  paper concedes composition drives part of the gap and the stratified version is *still*
  "planned for the next draft." For a fact in the abstract and conclusion, this is not
  optional. **OPEN since v1** (methods M5 / code M5). *Fix:* run and report the stratified
  (state × structure-age × cohort) KM.
- **The selection prediction is interpretation doing the work of evidence.** DDD p=0.67,
  absentee margin flat, yet "the margins move in the predicted direction and rule out the
  main alternative." A top referee reads this as confirmation bias. *Fix:* a powered test or
  explicit acknowledgement that the conditional margins are uninformative and the claim rests
  solely on volume.
- **Literature positioning gaps.** (i) Invokes misallocation/filtering but does not engage
  the housing-supply-elasticity literature (Saiz, Gyourko) that determines whether withheld
  homes bind on supply; (ii) Coven et al. (2024) — the natural quantitative benchmark on
  Prop 13 intergenerational misallocation — is cited only in passing; (iii) the stepped-up
  basis (IRC §1014) federal margin appears only in the magnitudes section but is a competing
  national retention mechanism deserving earlier treatment.
- **"Supply effect is real but deferred" is currently unfalsifiable within the data window**
  — a promissory note ("the data will eventually record whether"). This is the single biggest
  reason the causal half is not top-5-ready today, and it is a data-maturity problem no
  revision can fully fix. *Fix:* reframe so the accepted contribution does not depend on the
  deferred effect materializing.

## Journal fit

Not AER/QJE/JPE in current form. The measurement half could clear a top general-interest bar
*if* it were the whole paper and the welfare claims were earned, but as a combined paper the
fragile single-state causal half and the unobserved supply release cap it below the top-5
ceiling. Realistic targets: **Journal of Public Economics** or **Journal of Urban
Economics** (JUE especially for the turnover/supply angle). **AEJ:Economic Policy** is an
aspirational stretch and the best "top-general-interest-adjacent" shot, contingent on
splitting the paper or making the model do quantitative work. **Real Estate Economics /
JREFE** would take it comfortably but is below the author's stated ambition.

**Honest verdict:** a strong field paper (the measurement) plus a publishable-but-not-flagship
causal note (Prop 19), not one top-5 paper. The author should decide which paper to write.
