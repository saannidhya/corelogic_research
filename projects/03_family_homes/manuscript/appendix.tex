\section{Data Construction Details}\label{app:data}

\paragraph{Source and scope.} The CoreLogic Owner Transfer file compiles
recorded deeds from county recorder offices in all fifty states and the
District of Columbia. Our extract contains all record types---deeds of sale,
quitclaims, intra-family conveyances, trust transfers, and foreclosure
instruments. Coverage density is stable from 2007 onward; recording lags
truncate the file around August 2024. All annual statistics use 2007--2023;
the Proposition 19 cohort analyses use outcome windows that close by June 30,
2024.

\paragraph{Deduplication.} Multi-parcel transactions and re-recorded
corrections generate multiple rows per economic event. We keep one record per
parcel-date pair, preferring arm's-length category records and, within
category, the highest recorded consideration.

\paragraph{Classification.} The taxonomy in Section~\ref{sec:data} is
implemented with the recorder-derived interfamily indicator, the
arm's-length category code, recorded consideration, and
string features of the buyer and seller name fields (surname equality;
exact same-person matches on either buyer slot, which define the
same-person retitle class; trust keywords; estate, executor, probate, and
administrator keywords).
Missing name fields can move a potentially related-party deed from the surname-linked component into other interfamily deeds, which enter the main family measure. Missing names and imperfect full-name matching can also hide same-person or entity deeds, which Section~\ref{subsec:samep} removes from that measure; the upper bound adds them back. The measure therefore improves coverage of possible relatives with different surnames without validating every flagged relationship. Trust deeds and same-person retitles remain separate. National count tables retain a small residual of territory or military geographic codes in the derived panel; state-ranking figures and their U.S. reference line restrict to fifty states and DC. This distinction has negligible effect on the displayed rounded national share but matters for exact count reconciliation.

\paragraph{Occupant share of recipients.} Section~\ref{sec:prop19} measures occupancy with the mailing address on the deed. A recipient is counted as an occupant when the recipient's mailing street address equals the property address, among non-corporate recipients with both addresses recorded. This is an upper bound on owner occupancy, because a vendor may default a blank mailing address to the property address. Fill rates by period are in the notes to Table~\ref{tab:s4occupancy}. Time effects absorb common coverage changes; differential changes across states or transfer classes remain a measurement concern.

Among 2008--2018 cohorts, weighting family and market events to the same market state/cohort distribution gives sale rates of \TypeResaleFamilyCondo\ versus \TypeResaleMarketCondo\ percent for condominiums, \TypeResaleFamilyDuplex\ versus \TypeResaleMarketDuplex\ percent for duplexes, \TypeResaleFamilySF\ versus \TypeResaleMarketSF\ percent for single-family properties, and \TypeResaleFamilyTownhouse\ versus \TypeResaleMarketTownhouse\ percent for townhouses. These comparisons use the main family measure. They do not identify an effect of inheritance or distinguish retention from settlement and recording practices.

\section{Robustness Checks}\label{app:robustness}

\paragraph{Trust-inclusive measure.} The Section~\ref{sec:prop19} measure adds to the main family measure, without affidavits of death, only chain-verified trust-to-heir deeds. Adding every other trust-to-person deed gives the trust-inclusive measure, which Tables~\ref{tab:s4rush} and \ref{tab:s4hole} report beside the Section~\ref{sec:prop19} measure. Its shortfall is close to the Section~\ref{sec:prop19} measure's, but its rush is larger (Table~\ref{tab:s4hole}). Neither measure verifies an inheritance or a change in beneficial ownership, because direction and name fields cannot show it, and these comparisons cannot rule out substitution into legal vehicles rather than a change in housing use.
