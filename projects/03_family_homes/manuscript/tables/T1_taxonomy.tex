\begin{minipage}{0.97\textwidth}\vspace{2pt}{\scriptsize \textit{Notes:} Residential deed events, one per parcel and signing date, average of 2017--2023. Every event is in exactly one row of Panels A to D; memo rows repeat deeds already counted. ``Flagged'' means the vendor's interfamily indicator is set; the indicator does not verify kinship. A family transfer is a recorded deed that moves a home, with no sale, from one person, or that person's trust, to a different person, during life or at death. Direct deeds have no trust and no entity on either side, and the names show two distinct people. A deed through a trust takes title out of a trust and gives it to someone other than the trust's settlor: an earlier deed moved the home from a person into the trust and the trust deeds it to a different person with the same surname (chain-verified, ten-year look-back); or the trust names one person and deeds to a different person with that surname; or two trusts share a name and each names a person the other does not. Lifetime gifts and distributions after a death are not separated for trust deeds. An affidavit of death counts whether or not a trust is a party, so a home whose trust later deeds it to an heir can be counted twice. $^{a}$Identified only in California and Arizona; spouse deeds elsewhere sit in the other rows. $^{b}$The names cannot say whether the recipient is an heir or the settlor, or whether the parties are related; these two rows are the upper bound of the trust route. $^{c}$Into a trust for others, into a trust with no surname link, trust to trust with unclear names, and priced or arm's-length deeds from a trust to a person with another surname.}\end{minipage}
