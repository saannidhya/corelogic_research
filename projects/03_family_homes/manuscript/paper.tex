\begin{abstract}
\noindent Using 144 million residential deed events from 2007 to 2023,
this paper documents nearly 20 million within-family transfer deeds, one
for every 3.6 market sales. The family channel is especially prevalent in
California, is associated with older housing, and frequently precedes long
periods without a market sale. California's Proposition~19 provides evidence
on how inherited property-tax assessments shape this channel: transfers
surged 31\% above counterfactual before the deadline and then declined.
Synthetic-control specifications yield 2022--2023 gaps of 27--33\%; a
market-adjusted DiD corresponds to roughly \WelfareFlow\ missing deeds
annually, with substantial inferential uncertainty. The missing deeds have
not been shown to reappear as estate sales. A simple framework distinguishes
efficient family retention from retention induced by tax advantages. We
calibrate potential allocation gains using explicit assumptions about
tax wedges, distinct-property responses, succession delays, and persistence.
The exercise separates resource surplus from tax revenue and includes zero
gains when legal-title responses do not change allocation. \\
\textbf{JEL classification:} R21, R31, H22, D64 \\
\textbf{Keywords:} housing supply, intergenerational transfers, bequests, property taxes, Proposition 19
\end{abstract}
