\begin{abstract}
\noindent A large share of American homes change hands without a market. Using 144 million residential deed events from 2007 to 2023,
this paper provides national micro-level evidence on the prevalence and size of intra-family housing transfers. We find 17 family transfers per 100 market sales under a conservative same-surname definition (25 under a wider one), and this family channel is largest in California, where heirs could inherit a parent's low property-tax assessment.
 California's Proposition~19 provides a natural experiment to study how inherited property-tax assessments shape this channel: transfers
rose ahead of the February 2021 deadline, with the largest seasonally adjusted spike in the deadline month (although California transfers were already elevated before the measure reached the ballot), and then declined once it was implemented.
Net of California's market sales, a triple difference shows family
transfers decreased by \WelfareNetPercent\% in the post-reform period, roughly \WelfareFlow\ missing deeds
annually. A simple framework shows that when heirs inherit a below-market property-tax assessment along with
the house, as permitted by California’s Propositions 13, 58, and 193, families keep
homes that the market values more. Using the framework, we estimate the tax wedge and quantify the deadweight loss from the misallocation.
Our results suggest that family transfers are not outside the housing market but are one of its allocation mechanisms and property-tax rules can shift scarce homes from price-based matching to lineage-based retention.
