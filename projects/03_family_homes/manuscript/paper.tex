\begin{abstract}
\noindent A large share of American homes change hands without a market. Using 144 million residential deed events from 2007 to 2023,
this paper provides national micro-level evidence on the prevalence and size of intra-family housing transfers. We find 28 family transfers per 100 market sales, and this family channel increases with tax advantages and is associated with older housing stock.  
 California's Proposition~19 provides evidence
on how inherited property-tax assessments shape this channel: transfers
surged 31\% above counterfactual before the deadline and then declined.
Synthetic-control specifications yield 2022--2023 gaps of 27--33\%; a
market-adjusted DiD corresponds to roughly \WelfareFlow\ missing deeds
annually. The missing deeds have
not been shown to reappear as market sales. A simple framework shows that when heirs inherit a below-market property-tax assessment along with
the house, as permitted by California’s Propositions 13, 58, and 193, families keep
homes that the market values more.
Our results suggest that family transfers are not outside the housing market but are one of its allocation mechanisms and property-tax rules can shift scarce homes from price-based matching to lineage-based retention.
