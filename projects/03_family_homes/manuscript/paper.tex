\begin{abstract}
\noindent How many homes change hands without a sale, and what do families do when the tax code gives them a deadline? Using the CoreLogic extract of recorded residential deeds in the United States, 2007 to 2023, we find that deeds between two distinct people are \DeedNationalShare\ per 100 residential deed events nationally and \DeedCAShare\ in California. California's Proposition~19 ended the inherited property-tax assessment for most heirs who do not move in; to keep it, a deed had to be made by February 16, 2021. In the weeks before that date, California's family deeds ran \SIVMainJanFeb\ times a counterfactual, and deeds from a trust to an heir ran \SIVHeirJanFeb\ times. Spouse deeds, which the law does not touch, did not break at the deadline, although affidavits of death also dipped. Rushed deeds were resold less, but California's spouse deeds changed about as much, so that comparison does not isolate the reform. Afterwards, family deeds changed by $\SIVMainDDD$ log points relative to market sales, which is consistent with the reform but is shared by deeds the law does not touch and begins with the rise in mortgage rates in 2022. Attributing that fall to the reform puts the misallocation that the old exclusion created, and the reform removes, at about \$\WViLossCentral\ million a year, with a scenario range of \$\WViLossLow\ to \$\WViLossHigh\ million; without that attribution it is zero.
